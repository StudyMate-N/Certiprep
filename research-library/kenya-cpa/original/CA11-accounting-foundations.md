# CA11 Financial Accounting — Original Revision Primer

**Author:** Certiprep research project. **Status:** Original introductory primer; not a KASNEB-endorsed syllabus replacement.

## 1. Accounting equation
Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

### Example
A business begins with KES 200,000 cash invested by its owner. It buys equipment for KES 60,000 cash and supplies costing KES 15,000 on credit.

Cash: KES 140,000; equipment: KES 60,000; supplies: KES 15,000. Total assets: KES 215,000. Liabilities: KES 15,000. Equity: KES 200,000.

## 2. Double entry
Every transaction has equal debits and credits. An increase in an asset is normally debited; an increase in a liability is normally credited.

### Journal example
Equipment purchased for KES 60,000 cash:
- Dr Equipment KES 60,000
- Cr Cash KES 60,000

Supplies purchased on credit:
- Dr Supplies KES 15,000
- Cr Accounts payable KES 15,000

## 3. Accrual accounting
Revenue is recognised when earned and expenses when incurred, rather than simply when cash changes hands. Current recognition requirements must be checked against applicable IFRS standards.

### Worked exercise
A consulting business bills KES 80,000 for work completed in March but receives payment in April. Under the accrual basis, recognise KES 80,000 revenue and a receivable in March. April cash collection settles the receivable; it is not new revenue.

## 4. Practice
**Q1.** A company pays KES 12,000 rent in advance. Give the initial journal entry.

**Q2.** A company receives KES 25,000 against a previously issued invoice. What changes in the accounting equation?

**Q3.** A company has assets of KES 430,000 and liabilities of KES 175,000. Calculate equity.

## Answers
**A1.** Dr Prepaid rent KES 12,000; Cr Cash KES 12,000.

**A2.** Cash increases KES 25,000; trade receivables decrease KES 25,000. Total assets are unchanged.

**A3.** Equity = KES 430,000 - KES 175,000 = KES 255,000.
