## Scenario

A Kenyan manufacturing business produces 1,000 units. Standard material input is **2 kg per unit at KES 150/kg**. Actual material used is **2,100 kg at KES 145/kg**. Standard labour is **1.5 hours per unit at KES 400/hour**. Actual labour is **1,600 hours at KES 390/hour**. Assume all purchased material is used and that standards apply to actual output.

Calculate material price and usage variances; labour rate and efficiency variances; and reconcile the total actual material and labour cost with the standard cost allowed for actual output. Label each variance favourable (F) or adverse (A).

## Worked solution (independently recalculated arithmetic)

**Materials.** Standard quantity for actual output = 1,000 × 2 = **2,000 kg**. Standard material cost = 2,000 × 150 = **KES 300,000**. Actual material cost = 2,100 × 145 = **KES 304,500**.

- Price variance = AQ × (SP − AP) = 2,100 × (150 − 145) = **KES 10,500 F**.
- Usage variance = SP × (SQ − AQ) = 150 × (2,000 − 2,100) = **KES 15,000 A**.
- Net materials variance = 15,000 A − 10,500 F = **KES 4,500 A**, agreeing with 304,500 − 300,000.

**Labour.** Standard hours allowed = 1,000 × 1.5 = **1,500 hours**. Standard labour cost = 1,500 × 400 = **KES 600,000**. Actual labour cost = 1,600 × 390 = **KES 624,000**.

- Rate variance = AH × (SR − AR) = 1,600 × (400 − 390) = **KES 16,000 F**.
- Efficiency variance = SR × (SH − AH) = 400 × (1,500 − 1,600) = **KES 40,000 A**.
- Net labour variance = 40,000 A − 16,000 F = **KES 24,000 A**, agreeing with 624,000 − 600,000.

**Overall reconciliation:** Standard combined cost KES 900,000; actual combined cost KES 928,500; difference **KES 28,500 A**. Independently, (15,000 + 40,000) A − (10,500 + 16,000) F = **KES 28,500 A**.
