# CA34S4 — Original inventory costing practice (Kenyan public-sector setting)

**Original exercise; not an official KASNEB paper or PSASB course answer.** Arithmetic independently recalculated by a second computation. All data are fictional.

## Assumptions

The fictional county supplies department records homogeneous stores purchased in two batches. There are no opening stock differences, taxes, discounts, abnormal losses or other costs. Both issues occur after both batches are received. For this exercise only, use a periodic FIFO model or periodic weighted-average cost; all amounts KES. These simplified assumptions do not decide the appropriate measurement basis for inventories held for distribution without charge under IPSAS 12.

| Case | First batch | Second batch | Units issued |
|---|---|---|---:|
| A | 100 units at KES 80 | 60 units at KES 100 | 120 |
| B | 200 units at KES 150 | 100 units at KES 180 | 170 |

**Tasks:** For each case calculate total units available, total cost, ending units, periodic FIFO cost of units issued and ending stock, and periodic weighted-average cost per unit, issue cost and ending stock. Reconcile issued plus ending cost to total available cost. Explain why this illustration alone cannot establish IPSAS 12-compliant financial statement valuation.

## Worked solutions (recomputed)

**Case A:** Available 160 units, total KES 14,000; ending 40 units. FIFO issued 100×80 + 20×100 = **KES 10,000**; ending 40×100 = **KES 4,000**. Weighted average = 14,000/160 = **KES 87.50**; issued 120×87.50 = **KES 10,500**; ending 40×87.50 = **KES 3,500**. Both methods reconcile to KES 14,000.

**Case B:** Available 300 units, total KES 48,000; ending 130 units. FIFO issued 170×150 = **KES 25,500**; ending 30×150 + 100×180 = **KES 22,500**. Weighted average = 48,000/300 = **KES 160.00**; issued 170×160 = **KES 27,200**; ending 130×160 = **KES 20,800**. Both methods reconcile to KES 48,000.

**Interpretation:** These examples demonstrate cost-flow arithmetic. Public-sector inventories may require further classification, measurement and disclosure assessment under the applicable IPSAS and Kenya's transitional guidance. Learners must not automatically equate FIFO or weighted-average closing cost with the final reportable inventory balance.
