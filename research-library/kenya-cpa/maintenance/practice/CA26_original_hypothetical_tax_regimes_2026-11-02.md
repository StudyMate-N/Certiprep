# Original hypothetical tax-regime comparison — CA26 / CA34S1 supplemental practice

**Original teaching exercise; not extracted from a publisher, KASNEB paper, KRA, or World Bank recording. Not a statement of Kenyan tax law.** All tax rates are deliberately invented for this example. Independent academic review pending.

Two fictional businesses each have KES 4,000,000 in annual revenue. Business A has deductible costs of KES 3,500,000; Business B has costs of KES 2,000,000. Compare (i) a **hypothetical** 1% tax on gross revenue and (ii) a **hypothetical** 20% tax on positive accounting profit. Ignore other taxes and adjustments.

| Measure | A | B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 4,000,000 | 4,000,000 |
| Costs | 3,500,000 | 2,000,000 |
| Profit | 500,000 | 2,000,000 |
| 1% hypothetical turnover tax | 40,000 | 40,000 |
| 20% hypothetical profit tax | 100,000 | 400,000 |
| Turnover tax / profit | 8% | 2% |
| Profit tax / revenue | 2.5% | 10% |

**Discussion:** Equal revenue produces equal turnover-tax liabilities despite different margins. Relative to profit, the turnover levy is heavier for the low-margin business. Compare compliance simplicity with fairness, and explain why neither hypothetical rate should be used as current Kenya law. No claims about actual Kenyan tax eligibility, thresholds or rates are made.

**Arithmetic check:** 4,000,000×0.01=40,000; (4,000,000−3,500,000)×0.20=100,000; (4,000,000−2,000,000)×0.20=400,000; 40,000/500,000=8%; 40,000/2,000,000=2%; 100,000/4,000,000=2.5%; 400,000/4,000,000=10%.
