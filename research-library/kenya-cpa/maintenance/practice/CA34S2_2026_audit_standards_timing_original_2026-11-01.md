# Original CA34S2 / CA32 practice: audit-standard timing and assurance boundaries

Reviewed 1 November 2026. Fictional original cases, not KASNEB or IFAC questions. Selected dates and arithmetic checked; independent audit/assurance specialist review and current KASNEB exam cutoff remain pending.

## Case 1 — Which revised ISA edition is effective?
An audit team compares: A, financial year 1 January–31 December 2026; B, an annual period beginning 14 December 2026; C, an annual period beginning 1 January 2027. Under the IAASB's international effective-date rule, which periods fall within ISA 240 (Revised) and ISA 570 (Revised 2024) when no early adoption is assumed?

**Worked answer:** Both revisions apply to audits of financial statements for periods **beginning on or after 15 December 2026**. A and B begin earlier, so are not automatically within that gate. C begins later and is within the gate. Publication in the 2026 Handbook is not evidence that every 2026 audit period uses the revised requirements. Kenyan adoption and KASNEB sitting rules require separate confirmation.

## Case 2 — Going concern and contradictory evidence
A fictional entity has opening cash of KES 8 million, supportable forecast inflows of KES 20 million, and unavoidable payments of KES 33 million. Management forecasts a further KES 7 million equity injection based on an unsigned expression of interest. Calculate the gap before the proposed injection, and explain the assurance concern.

**Worked answer:** 8 + 20 − 33 = **−5 million KES**. Without firm funding the forecast has a **KES 5 million shortfall**. If KES 7 million were actually committed and timely, conditional headroom would be KES 2 million, but an unsigned expression of interest is not proof of available funding. The auditor should seek corroboration of financing, conditions, timing, forecasts and plausible downside cases. This is an illustration, not a complete audit opinion or reproduction of ISA 570 requirements.

## Case 3 — Reporting and assurance are different
A Kenyan public-interest entity claims that completing ICPAK's 2026 IFRS S1/S2 readiness assessment means it has complied with the disclosures and has obtained ISSA 5000 assurance. Assess.

**Worked answer:** **Incorrect.** ICPAK explicitly says readiness is preparatory and does not establish IFRS S1/S2 compliance. IFRS S1/S2 concern sustainability-related financial disclosure; ISSA 5000 is a separate assurance standard. IAASB's September 2026 adoption listing identifies Kenya via ICPAK, but effective local requirements, engagement date, level of assurance and regulator instructions still need checking. The portal's stated 17 October 2026 deadline had passed by 1 November 2026.
