# CA37WP — Original invoice-to-receivable practice (2026-11-02)

## Scenario

A fictional trading entity begins with no balance owed by customer A. On 4 November it invoices four service units at KES 8,500 each, on credit. On 6 November the customer pays KES 20,000 into the bank. On 10 November the seller issues a KES 2,000 credit note reducing the invoice. Ignore taxes, discounts, and other transactions.

**Questions:** Calculate the original invoice, post the three journal entries, compute the closing customer receivable and the net revenue after the credit note. Explain why a bank deposit must be matched to the recorded receipt rather than counted again as a new sale.

## Independently calculated answer

- Original invoice: 4 × KES 8,500 = **KES 34,000**.
- 4 November: Dr Trade receivables KES 34,000; Cr Revenue KES 34,000.
- 6 November: Dr Bank KES 20,000; Cr Trade receivables KES 20,000.
- 10 November: Dr Sales returns / revenue adjustment KES 2,000; Cr Trade receivables KES 2,000.
- Closing receivable: 34,000 − 20,000 − 2,000 = **KES 12,000**.
- Net revenue: 34,000 − 2,000 = **KES 32,000**.
- Bank-matching control: the KES 20,000 deposit corresponds to the existing receipt journal entry; matching it prevents duplicate revenue or cash recognition. Full bank reconciliation requires the complete bank statement and ledger, which this exercise does not provide.

**Checks:** 34,000 = 20,000 + 2,000 + 12,000; all three journal entries balance. Review date 2026-11-02 UTC. No external questions, publisher answers, screenshots or recordings copied. Independent professional/examiner review pending.
