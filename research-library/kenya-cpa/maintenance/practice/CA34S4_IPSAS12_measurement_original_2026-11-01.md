# CA34S4 / CA26 — Original IPSAS 12 inventory measurement practice
**Date:** 2026-11-01. **Status:** original fictional practice, arithmetic QA passed; IPSAS policy and current KASNEB-sitting mapping require independent academic review. Not a KASNEB/PSASB examination or official answer.

## Case 1 — Goods for distribution without charge
A fictional county agricultural service acquires **300 seedling kits at KES 240** and **200 kits at KES 270**. All kits are interchangeable, no ancillary costs/taxes arise, and both batches are received before issues. It issues **350 kits** to beneficiaries at no charge before the 30 June 2026 reporting date. At that reporting date, the replacement cost of the remaining identical kits is **KES 225 per kit**. Assume the kits remain serviceable and a reliable replacement market exists. The next reporting period it distributes **100 of the remaining kits** without charge. Use FIFO for the Kenya government-policy illustration. Assume no other price changes.

**Questions:** (a) Compute the purchase cost, FIFO cost of 350 issued kits, cost of remaining 150, reporting-date replacement-cost ceiling, write-down, and carrying amount. (b) Show illustrative inventory/expense journal entries, including the subsequent distribution. (c) Explain why a zero selling price is not a suitable NRV substitute.

**Checked solution:** Purchase cost = 300×240 + 200×270 = **KES 126,000**. FIFO issue = 300×240 + 50×270 = **KES 85,500**. Cost of 150 remaining = 150×270 = **KES 40,500**; reconciles to KES 126,000. Current replacement cost = 150×225 = **KES 33,750**. For free-distribution inventory, the lower of cost and current replacement cost is **KES 33,750**. Write-down = 40,500 − 33,750 = **KES 6,750**. Next-period distribution at the assumed unchanged carrying amount: 100×225 = **KES 22,500**; closing 50×225 = **KES 11,250**.

**Illustrative entries:** At purchase: Dr Inventory 126,000 / Cr Payables 126,000. At first issue: Dr Distribution/inventory expense 85,500 / Cr Inventory 85,500. At reporting-date write-down: Dr Inventory write-down expense 6,750 / Cr Inventory 6,750. Next-period issue: Dr Distribution expense 22,500 / Cr Inventory 22,500. Specific expense classification and timing under IPSAS 48, including its Kenya commencement and applicability, require separate review. The accounting policy and intended use must be established before treating these entries as production-ready.

**Interpretation:** IPSAS 12 uses lower of cost and current replacement cost for inventory intended for free or nominal-charge distribution. Zero proceeds do **not** mean its service potential should be written down to zero. Evidence of market replacement cost, control, and reporting date is required.

## Case 2 — Inventory held for commercial sale
A separate fictional public-sector trading unit buys **80 kits at KES 500** and **60 kits at KES 560**, then sells **90 kits**. At year-end, the remaining **50 kits** have net realizable value **KES 490 each**. Assume FIFO, no further costs, and that the quoted figure is already net of completion and selling costs.

**Checked solution:** Total acquisition cost = 80×500 + 60×560 = **KES 73,600**. FIFO cost sold = 80×500 + 10×560 = **KES 45,600**. Closing cost = 50×560 = **KES 28,000**. Closing NRV = 50×490 = **KES 24,500**. Lower of cost and NRV = **KES 24,500**, requiring a **KES 3,500** write-down. Check: 45,600 + 28,000 = 73,600 before write-down.

## Important Kenya policy distinction
The PSASB **IPSAS 12 Inventories Guideline** (official PSASB PDF; 31 pages; file listed on the PSASB directory as modified 27 April 2026) describes FIFO and weighted average as permitted general formulas. The **National Treasury's Accounting Policies of the Public Sector**, inventory section p.30–31, explicitly says the government has elected **FIFO**. Therefore earlier project practice presenting weighted-average results remains valid as a *mathematical comparison*, **not** as proof that weighted average is the adopted formula for Kenyan government reporting. Confirm the policy's precise entity and financial-year scope before certifying any public-entity answer.
